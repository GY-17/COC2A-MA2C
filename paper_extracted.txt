Multi-agent Reinforcement Learning based Flexible
Truck-drone Collaboration for Emergency Rescue
Yu Guan, Chunli Zhu, Jing Ye, Lei Chen, and Anastasios Kouvelas
Abstract—With the rapid development of Multi-agent Rein-
forcement Learning (MARL), implement the truck-drone collab-
oration could provide the efficient delivery of emergency supplies
in the dynamic and uncertain post-disaster rescue. However, the
sequential decision-making problem would naturally introduced
the issue of sparse rewards, which would causing the training
instability and the agents may select the selfish strategies. In
this work, we propose a Multi-Agent Advantage Actor-Critic
model with Multi-level Attribute Credit Assignment (MACA-
MA2C) method, which follows the Centralized Training with
Decentralized Execution (CTDE) framework. In the decentral-
ized actor network, we design a flexible collaboration mechanism
in which drones could continuously serve requests and land
on any available truck, while trucks could passively wait for
rendezvous without traveling to specific nodes.In the centralized
critic network, we integrate the Transformer architecture to
capture the complex spatiotemporal dependencies and introduce
the MACA mechanism to explicitly evaluate the marginal contri-
bution of each agent through counterfactual baselines. Extensive
simulation results demonstrate the feasibility and superiority
of our proposed method. Compared to MARL methods such
as MADDPG, MAPPO and MA2C, our MACA-MA2C reduces
the global average cost by 23.15% to 30.39% and improves the
average task completion time by 1.68% to 3.19%. Moreover, it
outperforms dynamic heuristic benchmarks by reducing average
operational costs by 9.18% to 17.50% and accelerating compu-
tation times by 25.97% to 73.71%. The source code is available
at: https://github.com/CZhu0066/MACA-MA2C.
Index Terms—Emergency rescue, Dynamic environment,
Truck-drone collaboration, MARL, Credit assignment.

Truck-drone collaboration has been extensively investigated
for a decade [8], which evolves from static and deterministic
model to recent dynamic and stochastic model. [9] reviewed
methods for drone-aided routing, which pointed out that the
inadequate research to addressing the dynamic and uncertain
factors of real-world applications. Previous work [10]–[12]
mainly focused on the optimization of deterministic routes,
which usually assume the location and demand of service
node as well as the status of road network are fixed and
already known before the optimization. For example, iterative
optimization method [13], Mixed Integer Programming (MIP)
model [14] and iterated greedy heuristic [15] have been
employed to address the large scale truck-drone collaboration
problem.
For the issue of uncertainty, [16] integrated the uncertainties
of wind direction and speed with a robust optimization model,
which is a key factor for the target of drone delivering small
parcels to remote islands. [17] investigate the issue of robust
vehicle routing with drones under uncertain demands and
truck travel times in humanitarian logistics, which utilize
uncertainty sets to capture random deviations and guarantee
solution feasibility. In [18]’s work, dynamic pickup problem
is formulated as a Markov Decision Process (MDP) and
then solved by a segment-based heuristic method. For the
fixed time delivery service, the uncertainty is handled with
sample average approximation and robust optimization [19].
For dynamic demand and varied drone/truck status, a rolling
horizon framework with adaptive large neighborhood search is
developed [20]. [21] develops a two-stage stochastic optimiza-
tion approach to handle the integrated scheduling problem.
To overcome the issue of dynamic and uncertain envi-
ronment, multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) based
approaches become noticeable since the occurrence of Al-
phaGo [22]. It has achieved great success in scenarios such
as large-scale traffic signal control [23], uncertain demand

response for urban logistic service [24] and Stochastic Vehicle
Routing Problem (SVRP) [25]. Typical algorithms including
Multi-Agent Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (MADDPG),
Multi-Agent Proximal Policy Optimization (MAPPO), and
Multi-Agent Advantage Actor-Critic (MA2C), in which cen-
tralized training with a designed global reward is conducted
for the decentralized execution inference to make optimized
decision under complicated scenarios. In the field of logistics,
centralized MARL has been proven to be effective for dynamic
multi-vehicle pickup and delivery problem [26]. Specifically
for the truck-drone collaboration, [27] developed an event-
driven framework for post-disaster rescue with MADDPG,
which is a typical and challenging long sequential task. Mean-
while, the environment typically generates scalar feedback
only at critical junctures such as successful delivery of supplies
or mission termination due to power depletion, leading to
the sparse rewards issue [28]. Under such delayed and sparse
global feedback signals, Markov decision processes struggle
to effectively address the credit assignment problem in task
execution [29]. That credit assignment leads to fluctuations in
policy gradients and low sample efficiency [30] and makes
agents highly susceptible to suboptimal local selfish policies,
which ultimately causing the collapse of the entire heteroge-
neous vehicle fleet’s coordination.
In this work, we propose a flexible truck-drone collab-
oration method for emergency rescue under dynamic en-
vironment, which is called as the Multi-Agent Advantage
Actor-Critic with Multi-level Attribute Credit Assignment
(MACA-MA2C). A MARL framework is designed for truck-
drone collaboration, integrating a multi-level credit assignment
mechanism that explicitly distinguishes individual agent con-
tributions, thereby effectively overcoming the bottlenecks of
sparse rewards in long sequential tasks. Extensive tests are
conducted based on the OpenAI Gym platform. The main
contributions of this work could be summarized as follows:
We propose a flexible truck-drone collaboration method
for the emergency rescue, i.e., the MACA-MA2C, which
includes the decentralized actor and centralized critic net-
work. Here, flexible cooperation strategies are designed
within the decentralized actor network, where the drone
agent could continuously serve request nodes and land
on any truck, and truck agent could passively wait for
the arriving of drones rather than traveling to rendezvous
point for collection.
•We leverage the Transformer architecture into the cen-
tralized critic network to capture global spatiotempo-
ral dependencies among heterogeneous agents. We also
integrated a Multi-level Attribute Credit Assignment
(MACA) mechanism that computing counterfactual base-
lines for both single agent and all agents, which could
effetively stabilizing the policy gradient under the cir-
cumstance of sparse rewards.
•Extensive simulation experiments validate the superiority
and robustness of the proposed method. Results indicate
that MACA-MA2C outperforms MARL baselines (e.g.,
MAPPO, MADDPG) and heuristic algorithms in mini-
mizing global costs and accelerating task completion.

II. RELATEDWORKS
In the framework of multi-agent system, agents interact,
collaborate, or compete to slove the complex and large-scale
task. In early Independent Q-Learning (IQL), each agent treats
other agents as part of the environment and update with
their local reward based on its own observation. In [31]’
work, they model a relatively large traffic network as a multi-
agent system and employed the Q-learning algorithm, which
achieving superior performance compared to traditional fixed-
timing methods. Besides, [32] proposes a deep Q-network
with Q-value transfer for adaptive multi-intersection signal
control and each agent searches the optimal strategy to control
its intersection. In the IQL regime, all agents are assumed
to make decisions at static environment, however, increasing
complexity of tasks & number of agents will inevitably result
in the non-convergence of training [33].
To overcome that challenge, Centralized Training with De-
centralized Execution (CTDE) framework has been proposed,
in which a centralized critic utilizes global information during
the training phase to eliminate environmental uncertainty,
while agents make decisions with their local observations dur-
ing the execution phase [34]. In the study of large-scale traffic
control, [35] validated that MA2C which incorporates neigh-
bors’ information to estimate the global advantage function
could effectively capture spatiotemporal dependencies, which
exhibit high stability compared to IQL. Similarly, MADDPG
approximates the global Q-value by inputting joint actions into
the critic to guide deterministic policies and shows remarkable
success in traffic signal control on urban road network [36]
and large-scale crowd path planning [37]. Meanwhile, MAPPO
utilizes a centralized value function to reduce policy gradient
variance within a trust region, which verify its effectiveness
on autonomous vehicles [38] and internet of vehicles system
[39].
Nevertheless, CTDE still faces strict action constrains com-
mon challenge when applying it to various complex real world
scenarios. For example, the strict physical constrains like
drone’s max battery, max capacity and road accessibility in the
emergency rescue [40]. Agents often attempt to explore large
action space, which may choose physically infeasible actions
and resulting in low search and training efficiency [41]. Then,
action masking has been proved useful which not only ensures
the safety of decision but also accelerates the algorithm’s
convergence speed by decreasing the search space. [42] has
proved action mask is a necessary condition for solving large-
scale constrained multi-agent problems, which can improve the
convergence speed of the algorithm under complex constraints
by several times. [27] directly masking the invalid actions at
the output layers of the actor network, therefore the agent can
sample within the feasible areas. The set up of action masking
not only ensures the safety of decision but also accelerates the
IEEETRANSACTIONSONINTELLIGENTTRANSPORTATIONSYSTEMS, VOL.XX, NO.XX, APRIL 2026 3
algorithm’s convergence speed by decreasing the search space.
However, collaboration mechanism in current work strictly
requires drones return to a fixed truck, whilst trucks are forced
to specific rendezvous nodes for drone collection.
In CTDE, the critic network needs to process the global state
that contains all agents information. Facing such information,
traditional critic Fully Connected (FC) Network struggles to
get effective solutions because it processes all input features
indiscriminately, which causing critical information to be
overwhelmed by a large amount of irrelevant data [43]. The
attention mechanism and Transformer architecture effectively
solve this problem by disregarding irrelevant details, thereby
allowing for better model generalization when facing unexpe-
rienced task changes [44]. Building on those advantages, [45]
incorporated multi-head attention mechanisms into the actor-
critic architecture to effectively capture spatial dependencies
in Unmanned aerial vehicles (UA Vs) and unmanned ground
vehicles (UGVs) cooperative routing problem. Specifically, the
self-attention mechanism distinguishes important information
from useless noise, thereby transforming the high-dimensional
and complex global state space into a compact global embed-
ding for the critic network [46]. Furthermore, [47] shows the
self-attention mechanism can improve the representation of
state and generalization of algorithm in cooperative navigation,
multi predator-prey, cooperative treasure collection and multi
speaker-listener environment.
In flexible truck-drone collaboration, a successful task typ-
ically depends on a long sequence of complex coordination
dynamics. For instance, while a truck stopping at a node
might appear to be an idle and negative behavior, it is
actually a crucial collaborative step to pick up a drone that
is about to run out of power. If only use global reward [48],
the system will have difficulty in distinguishing which key
action led to success or failure, which is primarily due to
the delayed and sparse environmental feedback. Otherwise,
if only using local reward [49], the agent will focus on the
quality of their own behavior and ignore the overall effect
of team collaboration. [50] shows that this ambiguity in the
feedback signal will result in high variance of the policy
gradient, which in turn will cause the training to fail to
converge or converge to a suboptimal solution. To quantify
each agent’s marginal contribution and effectively suppresses
gradient variance, [51] first proposed counterfactual multi-
agent policy gradient (COMA) with a counterfactual baseline.
In [52]’s work, they address the credit assignment and non-
stationary challenges by performing an additional counterfac-
tual trajectory based on the learned world mode, which enables
accurate credit allocation in long-range tasks. Recent research
begun to explore spatiotemporal joint credit allocation. For
example, [53] tackle cooperative MARL credit assignment
by introducing a MACA scheme that decomposes the team
advantage into individual, subgroup and global components
via attention-based counterfactual reasoning, which achieving
superior performance on challenging StarCraft benchmarks.
Furthermore, in the context of cooperative air-ground delivery,
[54] employ a hierarchical reinforcement learning framework
guided by internal reward mechanisms to address the credit
assignment problem address the credit assignment problem.
Despite those advances, how to addresses the sparse-reward
credit assignment with spatiotemporal dependency modeling in
a fully flexible truck-drone collaboration still remains further
investigation.
III. METHOD
In this work, we propose a MACA-MA2C framework
for the fully flexible truck-drone collaboration of emergency
rescue under dynamic request. Within MA2C, every truck and
drone is regarded as an agent, i.e., distributed actor; MACA
mechanism is introduced to the centralized critic network.
Overall framework of the proposed MACA-MA2C method is
illustrated in Fig. 1.
A. Problem statement
The target emergency rescue scenario is shown in the
upper right of Fig. 1, where trucks and drones are served
as transportation tools for post-disaster rescue request. They
have different maneuverability and load-carrying capacity, in
which the purpose of collaboration between drone and truck
is to collect goods from trucks and recharge on them. When
road has been damaged, request cannot be responded by truck,
while drones are also restricted by load demand. Besides,
power limitation forces drone to shuttle between destination
and nearby trucks for the task of load-carrying and charging.
Each request node has varied requirement and deadline, while
new request nodes are generated randomly at any time.
For simplification as well as the real-world drone take-
off/landing capability, we made following assumptions: (1)
Truck and drone agents have full access to the complete status
information of all pending service nodes. (2) The truck cannot
move to the next service node until all rendezvous drones have
been collected. (3) The time for goods loading and unloading
as well as the recharging time are ignored. (4) Drones can only
take off after the truck arrives at one node, and may return to
any available truck.

C. MACA Mechanism and network update
By calculating individual counterfactual and joint baselines,
MACA mechanism separately evaluates the marginal contri-
bution of the agent’s own action and the overall contribution
of the truck-drone collaborative team. The complete training
procedure is summarized in Algorithm 1.
Based on the global embeddingz s, the criticQ ϕ which
employs a linear decomposition architecture branches into
two distinct heads : a value head that outputs a state-value
scalarV(z s)and a weight head that generates an action-
weight vectorw(z s). The joint Q-value is formulated as:
Qϕ(zs,x) =V(z s) +w(zs)⊤x. Leveraging this architecture,
we explicitly construct three specific valuesa t,π t andc i,t to
get the advantage function.
The realized Q-value is computed using the actual executed
one-hot joint action vectora t, which reads,
Qϕ(zs,at) =V(z s) +w(zs)⊤at (13)
The joint baselineb Jnt is calculated using the joint policy

IV. RESULTS
A. Experimental Settings
To evaluate the performance of our algorithm, we adopt
the standard test instances from [55] to generate basic road
network information. Regarding the generation of dynamic
requests, we convert the static Agatz dataset into dynamic
scenarios following the dynamic update framework described
by [56] and introduce the concept of the dynamic request
rateρ dyn to reflect the proportion of dynamic request to total
request [57]. For instance,ρ dyn = 0.3refers to that 30%
of customer requests emerge randomly during the simulation
as dynamic requests. During training and evaluation, the
temporal window for dynamic requests generation is defined as
[Tstart,T end] = [α minT,α maxT], whereTdenotes the episode
horizon andα min,α max represent the lower and upper release
fractions, respectively. Furthermore, to demonstrate the ne-
cessity of truck-drone collaboration, we set the road damage
ratioη= 0.2, which indicating that 20% of the nodes are
damaged that impassable for trucks and require drones to
provide service.
Specifically, as shown in Fig. 2, we select the 1-center
class of instances, which mimics a circular city center where
customer locations follow a normal distribution around the
distribution depot. To ensure realistic travel times and battery
consumption, the dataset’s original dimensionless coordinates
are proportionally scaled to a15km×15km service area.
The map grid on the left side of the interface clearly marks
the spatial distribution of the depot and the customers, while
simultaneously displaying the details routes of the truck-
drone collaboration in real time. The right side is divided
into two main functional modules, (1) mission log module: it
dynamically tracks task allocation and execution progress; (2)
requests overview module: it clearly displays the completion
status of static and dynamic requests and simultaneously marks
the service deadlines for each requirement. When all rescue
tasks are completed, the interface will automatically switch
to the task completion page, which centrally displays key
statistical indicators such as the number of service nodes, total
reward, total cost, and delay penalties.
Following the relative speed assumption in [55] and the cost
parameters based on [58], we set the travel speed for trucks
and drones to 30 km/h and 60 km/h, and their operational costs
to 0.083 $/min and 0.021 $/min, respectively. Consistent with
[59], the travel distances between any two nodes are measured
using the Manhattan distance for trucks and the Euclidean
distance for drones. The service deadline for all request nodes,
regardless of being static or dynamic, are generated using the
standard methodology outlined in [60]. Parameters for both
the simulation environment and the algorithm configuration
are defined as in Table II.

C. Ablation Study
In this section, we perform the ablation study to evaluate the
contribution of each module, as shown in the Fig. 3 and Table
VI. Two models for comparison are MACA-MA2C (MLP)
and pure MA2C, in which the former one retains the MACA
mechanism but replaces the Transformer critic with an MLP.
MACA-MA2C (Transformer) demonstrates significant advan-
tages in both convergence efficiency and final performance,
which validating the necessity of dual optimization of mech-
anism and architecture. Specifically, the MACA mechanism
significantly reduces the variance of the policy gradient, which
enabling the model to exhibit faster convergence and higher
stability in the early stages of training. Moreover, the average
total cost is reduced from 217.60 to 203.23, a reduction of
approximately 6.6%. Besides, the Transformer Critic with
multi-head self-attention mechanism breaks through the repre-
sentation bottleneck of MLPs, which could accurately capture
the complex spatiotemporal dependencies between trucks and
drones. It further reduces the average total cost to 166.60,
which achieving a performance improvement of approximately
18.0% compared to the MACA-MA2C (MLP) and 23.4%
compared to the MA2C.

In addition, we investigate the joint impacts of the road
damage ratioηand the degree of dynamismρ dyn on the
average total cost, as shown in Fig. 5 (d). Results show that
the average total cost monotonically decreases with increasing
environmental complexity, which is ranging from 234.9 in
the scenario (η= 0.1,ρ dyn = 0.2) to 101.1 in the extreme
scenario (η= 0.7,ρ dyn = 0.7). This cost evolution reflects
the dynamic adjustment of vehicle selection at the strategic
level. Given that the operational cost of drones (0.21/min) is
substantially lower than that of trucks (0.83/min), higherη
values restrict ground accessibility, while higherρ dyn requires
greater response flexibility. Therefore, the system will tend to
reduce its reliance on trucks and instead optimize delivery
routes by increasing the proportion of drone missions. The
low-cost in the upper-right region of the heatmap demonstrates
that the proposed flexible collaboration mechanism can fully
tap the efficiency of air transport in harsh environments and
achieve a balance between the overall robustness and economy
of the system.
V. CONCLUSION
In this paper, we proposed a MACA-MA2C framework
to address the challenging truck-drone collaborative routing
problem for emergency rescue under dynamic and uncertain
environments. We established a flexible collaboration method
that allows drones to continuously serve requests and be
recovered by any truck, while trucks just passively wait for
rendezvous without traveling to specific points. This design
significantly enhances the system’s adaptability to road dam-
age environment and dynamic service requests. Extensive
simulation results validated the superiority of our method,
achieving the global average cost savings by about 23.15%
compared to MA2C, about 30.39% compared to MAPPO
and about 27.38% compared to MADDPG, while the average
episode time was shortened by up to 3.19%. Furthermore, the
flexibility analysis confirmed the effectiveness of the proposed
mechanism, which reduced the total cost, episode time and
delay penalty by 28.30%, 21.52% and 34.5%, respectively.
Despite those contributions in efficiency and flexibility, the
current validation is primarily conducted within a simulated
environment with randomly distributed nodes based on Ope-
nAI Gym. Future work will focus on extending the algorithm
to address partially observable environments with noisy or
constrained communication channels.
